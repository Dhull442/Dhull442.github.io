Agentic Thoughts

Nov 7, 2025

With the emergence of Agentic AI and workflows, I decided to dive a bit into understanding what it might change and how it will affect the software industry in general. It was obvious from the beggingin that this is a great tool that can revolutionize the software industry but I had very little idea about the actual level of impact it will create. Here are my thoughts after researching a bit. A lot of the thoughts are philosophical and have potential to be wrong. These thoughts emerged one night and I couldn’t stop from writing them down, so if you feel something is interseting or find something I might have mis thought, please come forward and send me a message, I’d love to hear your thoughts.

Here’s What I have learned: AI agents are tools that can solve specific problems within their own limited context and understanding.

Agents as interns#

While reading LangChain’s documentation, I came across this beautiful line: “Only spawn an AI agent for stuff that your best intern could do.” What does it mean and Why does this matter? Think about it, Interns are new to the company and usually don’t have the time and the experience to fully grasp the entire context of how things operate in the company. So while they are good at performing well on a specific task assigned to them, the maximum output they can provide is very contextual and limited. That being said, Interns are not tools and deserve pay and respect (end upaid internships!).

AI agents work the same way. They excel at focused, well-defined tasks but struggle with broader understanding. This is why the idea that “I’ll just build an AI agent to handle everything” is unrealistic.

What this means for Jobs?#

It’s undeniable that this technology is going to change the market. Tasks like basic refactoring and debugging which were often handled by junior engineers and interns would be impacted first. While this change is definitely stressful, as all changes are, I think this is a transition and not a permanent replacement of workforce by AI. While Agentic workflows might be good at doing work that required less context and was often redundant, it needs a lot of context and understanding to build something useful with these tools. Let me explain!

Technical Transitions throughout the history#

Look at it from the business perspective, Picture the pre-computers era, every thing had to be done manually. All the calculations were done by hand in ledgers, which meant tallying up and calculating was a job. You’d have to hire a photographer to take a photo. You’d need a team of artists to create a presentation, which meant shelling out more money, time and resrocues for the same task. Then came the computers, which gave everyone the ability to generate their own presentations, click their own pictures, do multiple calculations at once. Those teams just adding and tallying up the numbers was essentially gone. Our systems got more streamlined. But did it solve all our problems? No. Not even close.

Now from the tech perspective, Early computers could only handle basic tasks. So we built better software that could do more complex work, leverage better hardware, solve harder problems. The work evolved, but we discovered a newer limit. Instead of managing a team doing calculations, you were now managing developers building software to solve business problems.

But even then, there was still a bottleneck. A lot of time was wasted on just perfectioning what you had in mind and what the team was able to come up with given time, compute and cost constraints. The system improved, but someone still had to write the code and iterate, and it needed to happen fast to meet the dealdines.

Now we’re in the era of gen AI, You have programs that can do intern-level work. Does that mean associate jobs are gone? Yes but No. You’ve just automated the generation but there’s still debugging, integration, and refinement that needs human judgment. So this reduced the development and iteration time, which meant you can solve more problems in shorter times. But this doesn’t mean that we will solve all existing human problems.

The Fundamental Truth#

Understand that the goal of any software/business is to solve a problem faced by another entity. Everything that we do is, in the end, to satisfy the basic human needs that keep us alive.

All the different business came into effect because we, collectively thought that instead of everyone doing everything, we could have specializations with people working on differnt tasks. Businesses evolved to solve these problems at scale. As population and problem size grew, the businesses were always thriving because transactions needed to happen. Let’s break it down even further. Do you think humans could just stop eating? no, so food industry will always exist. Can a food industry survive if all the underlying tech died? No. Evolution of technology follows the business needs, not the other way round. And when there are business problems there would exist humans to sovle those problems and technology is merely a tool to enhance the efficiency, which improves the accessibility.

The Numbers Game#

Now each person has a limit on their working capacity. Maybe you can work 16-hour days or be 30% more productive than average. That creates individual advantages, but the average stays roughly constant. Most people work about 6 productive hours out of an 8-hour shift.

So now these goals or problems that keep on coming in business would mean that people would need to solve them and since the problem size generally grows with the population, the number bussiness size / people working woudl stay more or less constant.

Evolution of Business Complexity#

The above theory implies that businesses stay the same, but since the next question is if we cut the jobs that are now beign done by AI or Agents, wouldn’t that mean that this business size/ people working would increase because now the same work can be done by less people. Each person with 4 agents could decrease the size by liek 4? That is correct. That would happen. But did you consider that a business would not grow it’x complexity? Let’s say you aim to solve a particular problem, you would more or less operate in a loop, where you aim for a problem, you write a basic theory of solving it, you bottstrap your resources to solve it quikcly then you get some results, now these results would lead to business outcomes or more plainly money, which will lead to more resources. So now your original solution was good for solving a subproblem of your original aim but not really the most perfect solution to the problem you initially wanted to sovle. So you go ahead and increase the complexity of this problem, and continue the same loop. This would mean that the overall complexity of your business has increased. And if let’s say you have reached the epitome of your problem or have theorectically solved the problem, you would be more or less the king of the hill and everyone would consider that particular problem to be solved for humanity. Which is rarely the case. We as humans are continuously evolving and so are our problems, that means even if you think you built the most supreme system today, it is not going to fulfil the necessaties of tomorrow. Which means you are still in the loop. With ai agents this bootstrapping part is more or less solved, sicne they can give you more output for less resrouces, so the compleixty of the solution would be higher from the get go and on each step the delta of complexity would be greator, but my hypothesis is that it will never reach a point where there is no need for any more deltas. (why?)

Way Ahead#

So since the overall complexity of the problems that we solve and the solutions that we come up with keeps on increasing. Now when initially we needed people who could do math then we needed people who could code, now we need people who could create these ai agents. The people remain the same, but the output or the task that they do will evolve. So the only thing to smoothen this transition? grow and learn what is now the current baseline. Previously you would have been rewarded to write basic code, now you would be given the same reward if you could build an ai agent. So All we need to do it learn and embrace the change. Which is what evolution is in the entierely. those who evolve progress and tho who stick too much to their boxes stay the same. I am in no position to preach you about always growing yourself, learning stuff and all that. No, I propose my own thoughts about the current tech space changes and what we have coming in the future. While we don’t know what new complexer problems we might solve, it’s still better to adapt to the change than overcome it. If you fear your job may be made redundant, it’s better to learn newer skills than staying at the same place. Now this problem is faced more by people that are in the lower parts of the solution chain. But if you go higher, you’d see that when a lower level is removed, you can still keep on doing what you did but now your impact is smaller as compared to earlier.

It’s harder to actually learn when thigns get complex, but the harder the thing is, the more impact it is going to carry.
